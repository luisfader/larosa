*Exactly one year after the war, we meet Rosa in the tower of Our Utopia. The leader of the rebels, the school's history teacher and the most-voted member of the assembly. But today, for the first time, she talks about herself, about her heart.*

![](img/rosa-1.webp)

**Journalist:** Rosa, everyone knows you as a warrior. Today we'd like to talk with you about feelings. Are you ready?

**Rosa:** (Laughs.) This is harder than cleaning a gun. But let's try. In the desert I learned one thing: a heart that stays silent also explodes one day. I've been silent long enough.

**Journalist:** What did you feel when you escaped from the mines of Necrology?

**Rosa:** Rage. Only rage. Not even fear; fear is a luxury, a feeling for people with full stomachs. In the mines we fell asleep listening to the children cough. The night we escaped, I swore to myself that I would never again go inside any walls, that I would never again bow my head to anyone. Rage kept me on my feet for two years. But you can't live on rage; on rage you can only survive.

![](img/rosa-2.webp)

**Journalist:** Your first meeting with Robert wasn't very romantic...

**Rosa:** Romantic? I pulled him out of the savages' hands, grabbed him by the collar and put him on my horse! (Laughs.) In the camp he told us about walls, about rules, about "everyone together". And I told him it was "a pretty fairy tale". Because I was afraid. If I believed him, I would have to break my oath.

**Journalist:** But you gave him a desert rose.

**Rosa:** Yes. (She is silent for a moment.) Everything I couldn't tell him I put inside that little crystal. I told him "go now", but I left a piece of my heart in his hand. At the mouth of the canyon I watched him go until he disappeared behind the cloud of dust. I wanted him to look back, and at the same time I was afraid he would. Whether he looked or not, I don't know. He never told me.

![](img/rosa-3.webp)

**Journalist:** Then came that night of rain.

**Rosa:** That night I thought I was going to die. I was wounded, bleeding, my people scattered in the mud. And he came. Two hundred kilometres, alone, when nobody believed him. When I opened my eyes, first I saw a turquoise ceiling and then him: asleep in a chair, unshaven, exhausted. At that moment I understood that walls don't imprison people. Sometimes, inside a wall, someone keeps watch for you.

![](img/rosa-4.webp)

**Journalist:** And you stayed in the fortress.

**Rosa:** I gave everyone other reasons. That I couldn't walk, that the children were going to school, that Anselmo had seen the greenhouses... All of them were true. But the real reason I told no one. Not even myself. In the evenings we met in the tower; we talked for hours and then suddenly fell silent. Those silences... (She smiles.) When a woman learns to be silent beside a man, there's nothing more to be done.

![](img/rosa-5.webp)

**Journalist:** And the duel? Luis and Robert fought over you.

**Rosa:** No. Not over me, but over their own pride. I am not a prize; I said it aloud in the square. But when Robert's blood ran over my hands, I couldn't stay silent any longer. I chose him in front of everyone. Not because he promised me a fortress, but because he was honest enough to admit he had lost. A man's strength doesn't show when he wins, but when he loses.

**Journalist:** What do you feel for Luis?

**Rosa:** (A long silence. Her hand goes to her neck, to the scarf that is no longer there.) Luis was the man who dreamed the stones of this fortress. He was jealous, he was wounded, he trusted the wrong people. But in the end he came back and stayed beneath his own arch to save us. The colourful scarf he threw down in the square on the morning of the duel, I kept without telling anyone. I don't know why. Maybe because I wanted to forgive him. Or maybe to remind myself that you shouldn't judge a person only by their worst day. Now that scarf is on his headstone. Every morning, on my way to school, I walk past it and repeat his last words to myself: "Make it wider." Those words apply to our hearts too.

![](img/rosa-6.webp)

**Journalist:** At the school you teach history. What do you tell the children?

**Rosa:** The history of those who lost. Everyone already knows the history of those who won. But lately I also tell something else: the history of the dreamers. I learned that from Robert. A fortress is first born in someone's head. So is a love.

![](img/rosa-7.webp)

**Journalist:** Are you still afraid?

**Rosa:** Of course. Necrology is still there; someone is still giving weapons to the savages. But my fear has changed. Before, I wasn't afraid because I had nothing to lose. Now I have a lot to lose, and that's why I'm afraid. It's a better fear. It teaches you to keep watch, to love, to listen to the rain at night through the window.

![](img/rosa-8.webp)

**Journalist:** Last question: what is love, for you?

**Rosa:** I once told Robert that nobody built the desert rose. That wind and salt work for centuries and it opens on its own. About the fortress I was wrong: hundreds of hands built it. But about love I was right. Nobody builds love. You just wait for the rain. And one night, it rains in the desert.

*(She looks out of the window at the turquoise roofs and laughs that whole, limitless, childlike laugh.)*

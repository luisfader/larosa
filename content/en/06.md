The savages arrived a week later, at sunset. They brought strange, menacing catapults; with them they meant to hurl barrels of burning tar at the turquoise walls of the fortress.

"We can't let them fire the catapults," said Robert. His voice was hoarse. "Kennet, to the north tower. Martín, to the south tower. Uncover the weapons."

"Already?" Martín's eyes lit up. "Great!"

In the towers there were weapons Robert had never wanted to use: rocket launchers. Kennet lifted the tarpaulin, aimed at the distant catapult and pulled the trigger. The catapult flew to pieces. Kennet felt a fleeting joy. Then, seeing what was happening, he was seized by horror.

Martín had not aimed at the catapults, but at the savages themselves.

"Stop!" shouted Robert.

The savages withdrew. In the fortress people began to shout with joy, but Robert silenced them.

"This is no time to celebrate. Today is a very sad day for me. We didn't achieve peace, and we had to use weapons we didn't want to use."

Rosa was beside him. She looked at Robert; a long, thoughtful look.

"I'm going to see the wounded," she said.

"They're the enemy," Martín sneered. "They came to burn our walls."

"Two weeks ago I was their enemy too," said Rosa coldly. "Anselmo, get your bag."

Kennet, Sasha and Luis went with them. On the plain there was a terrible sight: dozens of wounded, people groaning and shaking with fear. The work of Martín's rocket. *What a wretch*, thought Kennet.

Sasha bent over one of the wounded and asked him why they had attacked.

"Because it's a beautiful fortress," said the man, in pain. "We never had anything beautiful."

Sasha was about to say something angry, but Rosa put a hand on her shoulder.

"I know this man," said Rosa. "From the mines of Necrology. He escaped the same day I did, but in another direction. We went to the desert. He went with Juan. That's the only difference between us."

That night, when Robert learned that some of the wounded savages had agreed to stay in the fortress, he found Rosa at the top of the tower.

"Today you did what I should have done," he said.

"And on the night of the rain you did what I should have done," said Rosa. "You saved my people. So we're even."

"I don't want to be even," said Robert. His own voice sounded strange to him.

Rosa was silent for a long time. Then, slowly, very slowly, she rested her head on his shoulder. Neither of them spoke. Below, the windows of the fortress were lighting up one by one.

That month the first visitors came to the fortress. At the gate, a slender woman with blue eyes, in a black dress embroidered with gold, her golden blonde hair falling in soft waves over her shoulders, lifted her veil. Her name was **Oxana**. She was a singer and she wanted to give a concert.

Robert distrusted her; when he learned she came from Necrology, he distrusted her even more. But Rosa recognised her.

"She sang in the underground halls," she said. "For the rich. But one night, the night before the uprising, she sang for us too, for the people from the mines. In secret. They flogged her for it."

Oxana's eyes filled with tears. Robert opened the gate.

When that gate opened, Martín looked at Oxana as if struck by lightning. So did Kennet. And Sasha saw how Kennet looked at Oxana and felt something break inside her.
